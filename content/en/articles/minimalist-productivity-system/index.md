## The Trap of Productivity Optimization

There's a special kind of procrastination that masquerades as productivity: endlessly optimizing your system instead of doing the work. I've rebuilt my task manager four times, tried every major note-taking app, and read more books about productivity than I've care to admit. The result was an elaborate system that required constant maintenance and gave me anxiety just looking at it.

The fix wasn't a better tool. It was fewer tools.

## What I Actually Use

My current system has three components:

1. **A weekly plan written on Sunday evening.** Three to five outcomes I want to achieve by the following Friday. Not tasks — outcomes. "Finish the API integration" rather than "write auth middleware, write tests, update docs."
2. **A daily note.** Each morning I open a plain text file named with the date and write three things I'll do today. No categories, no priorities, no tags. Just three things.
3. **A capture inbox.** One place — a single app, always open — where anything that needs attention goes immediately. I process it during my weekly review.

## The Principles Behind It

- **Fewer decisions.** Every system choice you make at task-management time is attention stolen from the work itself.
- **Outcomes over tasks.** Tasks are inputs; outcomes are what you actually care about. Keeping outcomes front and center prevents busy-work from filling the day.
- **Weekly rhythm over daily pressure.** Committing to outcomes over a week rather than rigid daily schedules gives you resilience when life interrupts.

## What I Dropped

I stopped using: a dedicated task manager, a Zettelkasten, a habit tracker, a time-blocking calendar, and a "second brain." Each had value in isolation. Together, they were a second job.

Simple systems get used. Complex ones get abandoned.
