## It Starts With the Bean

Every brewing method amplifies certain characteristics of the coffee you're using. A bright, lightly roasted Ethiopian natural will taste entirely different through a Chemex than through an espresso machine — not because one is better, but because each method extracts differently. Understanding that relationship is more useful than chasing the "best" method.

## The Main Contenders

### French Press
**Profile:** Full-bodied, rich, slightly silty.

The metal filter lets oils and fine particles through, producing a heavier cup than paper-filtered methods. Forgiving with grind size and timing. Best with medium-to-dark roasts where you want texture and weight.

### Pour-Over (Chemex, V60, Kalita)
**Profile:** Clean, bright, nuanced.

Paper filters trap oils and sediment, letting delicate aromatics shine. Requires more attention — grind size, water temperature, and pour pattern all matter. Ideal for light-roasted single origins where you want to taste the terroir.

### AeroPress
**Profile:** Flexible — anywhere from espresso-like concentrate to a clean, light cup.

The most versatile brewer on this list. Short brew time, forgiving of variables, and easy to clean. An excellent travel companion and a surprisingly capable home brewer.

### Espresso
**Profile:** Concentrated, intense, with crema.

High pressure forces water through finely ground coffee in 25–30 seconds. The starting point for cappuccinos, lattes, and flat whites. Requires a proper machine and grinder — the equipment investment is real.

## My Honest Recommendation

If you want one brewer: **AeroPress**. If you're a morning ritual person who loves process: **pour-over**. If you drink mostly milk drinks: **espresso**, but budget for the grinder before the machine.

Freshly ground beans matter more than the brewer. Start there.
