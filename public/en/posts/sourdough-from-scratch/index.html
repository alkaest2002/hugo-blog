Sourdough From Scratch

Making sourdough bread from a starter you built yourself is slow, a little unpredictable, and completely worth it.

Table of Contents

  • The Bread Formula
  • Bulk Fermentation and Shaping

Starting the Starter

The first thing to understand about sourdough is that it’s not really about bread — it’s about building a living culture of wild yeast and bacteria. Before you bake anything, you need a starter: a mixture of flour and water that you feed daily until it becomes reliably active.

My recipe is simple. Mix 50g of whole wheat flour with 50g of room-temperature water in a clean jar. Cover loosely and leave it at room temperature. Each day, discard all but 50g of the mixture, then feed it with 50g flour and 50g water. After five to seven days you’ll see consistent bubbling and a pleasantly sour, almost fruity smell. Your starter is ready.

The Bread Formula

Once your starter is active, a basic country loaf uses:

  • 450g bread flour
  • 50g whole wheat flour
  • 375g water (75% hydration)
  • 9g salt
  • 100g active starter

Mix the flour and 340g of water until no dry patches remain, then rest for 30 minutes (this is the autolyse). Add the starter and salt with the remaining water, incorporating everything until the dough feels cohesive.

Bulk Fermentation and Shaping

Over the next four hours at room temperature, perform four sets of stretch-and-folds spaced 30 minutes apart. The dough will become noticeably stronger and more elastic. Shape it, place it seam-side up in a well-floured banneton, then refrigerate overnight — anywhere from 10 to 16 hours.

Baking

Preheat your oven to 500°F with a Dutch oven inside. Tip the cold dough into the hot pot, score the surface with a sharp blade, cover, and bake for 20 minutes. Remove the lid and bake another 20–25 minutes until deeply brown.

The crust will sing as it cools. Wait at least an hour before cutting in.
