My Week in Kyoto

A week wandering through Kyoto's ancient streets, bamboo groves, and tea houses left me rethinking what a city can be.

Table of Contents
  • Arriving in the Old Capital
  • What I Actually Did
  • What Surprised Me

Arriving in the Old Capital

Kyoto doesn’t announce itself the way Tokyo does. There’s no neon overload, no thunderous Shinkansen interchange to overwhelm you on arrival. The city reveals itself slowly — a moss-covered stone lantern here, a persimmon tree leaning over a clay wall there. I arrived on a Tuesday in April, one week before peak cherry blossom crowds, and the timing turned out to be perfect.

My base was a machiya guesthouse in the Nishiki neighborhood, a narrow wooden townhouse with paper screens, a tiny inner garden, and the faint smell of old cedar. It cost roughly the same as a mid-range hotel but felt like a completely different country.

What I Actually Did

No single itinerary can cover Kyoto, so I stopped trying to be comprehensive and picked a different district each day.

  • Arashiyama — The bamboo grove is as surreal as the photos suggest, though you need to arrive before 8 a.m. to experience it without shoulder-to-shoulder crowds.
  • Fushimi Inari — Ten thousand torii gates winding up a forested mountain. I hiked to the summit in two hours and had the upper trails almost to myself.
  • Gion — Cobblestoned Hanamikoji Street at dusk, where I spotted two maiko hurrying between engagements.
  • Philosopher’s Path — A canal-side walk lined with cherry trees, best with a canned coffee and no particular destination.

What Surprised Me

The food was the genuine revelation. I’d expected temple food and kaiseki, but the casual lunch counters — a bowl of tofu dengaku here, a soba set there — were just as transcendent. I also hadn’t expected Kyoto to be so walkable, or so quiet after 9 p.m.

I’ll go back. Probably in autumn, when the maples turn.
