Training for My First Marathon

Eighteen weeks of early mornings, long runs, and a whole lot of learning. Here's what actually prepared me to run 26.2 miles.

Table of Contents
  • Why I Signed Up
  • The Training Plan
  • What I Got Wrong
  • Race Day

Why I Signed Up

I’d been running casually for two years — a few miles here, a 10K there — but never with a real goal. Signing up for a marathon was mostly an act of self-coercion. If I’d paid for a race, I’d actually train for it. The logic proved sound.

The Training Plan

I followed a modified version of Hal Higdon’s Novice 1 plan, which peaks at a 20-mile long run and tops out around 40 miles per week. A few things I learned the hard way:

  • Run your easy runs easy. This is harder than it sounds. Most beginners (me included) run too fast on recovery days, digging a fatigue hole they can’t climb out of.
  • The long run is non-negotiable. You can miss a Tuesday tempo run and be fine. Miss three consecutive long runs and your fitness foundation starts to crack.
  • Fueling is a skill. I practiced taking gels at mile 5, 10, and 15 during training runs so race day wouldn’t be an experiment.

What I Got Wrong

I underestimated recovery. Sleep, protein, and easy walking days aren’t soft — they’re where adaptation happens. I also ignored hip flexibility until week 12, when a tight IT band sidelined me for nine days.

Race Day

The first 18 miles felt controlled. Miles 19–22 were the wall everyone warned me about — not dramatic, just heavy and slow. Miles 23–26 were something else entirely: a strange mix of exhaustion and clarity, legs running on whatever fuel is left after glycogen.

Crossing the finish line felt quieter than I expected. Not euphoric — just done, in the best possible way.

Final time: 4:11:34. I’m already thinking about next year.
